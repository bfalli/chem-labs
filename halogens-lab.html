The Activity of the Nonmetals: virtual lab

Compare the halogens to find which is most active, and whether nonmetal activity increases up or down the family. Work through both parts here, and record what you see on your lab report.

Start by putting on your safety goggles. Then click a bottle on the shelf to pick it up.

Part I: reaction with chlorine water

  1. Take three clean test tubes. Label them NaF, NaBr, and KI, then add 20 drops of each solution to its tube. Each solution stands in for its halogen.
  2. Add 20 drops (1.0 mL) of hexane to each tube. Hexane evaporates fast, so don’t leave it open.
  3. Only after the hexane is in, add 5 drops of chlorine water to each tube. Tap the tube to mix, and watch the color in the top layer.
  4. Pour each tube into the halogen waste, then rinse it three times.

How to read the result

This is a replacement reaction. If chlorine is more active than the halogen in the compound, it replaces it, and a color appears in the top layer. Color means that halogen is less active than chlorine. No color means it is more active.

Part II: reaction with Ca(NO₃)₂

  1. Use four clean large test tubes. Label them NaF, NaCl, NaBr, and KI.
  2. Measure 5.0 mL of each solution into its tube. The first tube is your guide: use its height to judge 5.0 mL in the other three.
  3. Add 10 to 15 drops of 0.1 M Ca(NO₃)₂ to each tube. Record what you see. The reaction may be subtle. If nothing happens, write “NO RXN.”
  4. Pour out the tubes, then rinse each one three times.

To pour, pick up a solution bottle, then hold the mouse button (or your finger) down on a tube. Each tick adds 0.1 mL, and the readout under the tube shows the total.
